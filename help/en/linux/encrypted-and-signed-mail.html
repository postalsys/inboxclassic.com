Encrypted and signed mail

The bar above a message says whether it is signed by its sender and whether it was encrypted to you.

A valid signature confirms that the content has not changed since it was signed. To confirm who signed it, also verify who owns the key. An encrypted message can only be read with a matching private key.

The bar above the message gives the message as a whole:

ColorWhat it means
GreenSigned by the sender, with a key you verified.
BlueSigned by the sender with a key you have not verified, or encrypted to you.
AmberSomething is not right: the signature does not match, someone other than the sender signed, or a part could not be read.
GrayNot signed, or the signature could not be checked, for example because its key is missing.

A message whose parts are protected differently shows each part in a frame of its own, with its own label. The bar then counts the parts of each kind. Click a label, or Details on the bar, to see who signed each part, with which key and when.

Only the bar and the labels above the frames are written by Inbox Classic. A sender can draw anything inside their own message, including something that looks like a label. Look at the bar.

Pictures from the internet in an encrypted part load only when you click Download Pictures on the bar, every time. Its label then says that pictures were loaded.

Pictures and PDF files attached to a part are shown inside that part’s frame, never in another part’s. Decrypted previews stay in memory. Opening an attachment in another app writes an unencrypted temporary copy; saving writes a copy where you choose.

In the list of attached files, a lock marks each file of an encrypted part. Point at a file to see which part it came from.

When you reply or forward, only what was shown is quoted.

In the message list, a lock marks an encrypted message and a seal a signed one. These marks do not verify signature validity or key ownership. After successful decryption and indexing, the list can show the protected subject and first words. Otherwise it shows the placeholder; new-mail notifications always show Encrypted message.

With Search in encrypted messages on under Main Menu > Preferences > Reading, available private keys allow decrypted text and protected subjects to enter the local search index, unencrypted. Turning it off removes these derivatives and restores outer subjects. AI agents see only outer details, without decrypted text, protected subjects or attachments.
