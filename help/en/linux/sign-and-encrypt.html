Sign and encrypt a message

Sign a message to protect its integrity, and encrypt its content for you and its recipients.

A valid signature shows whether the signed content has changed. Recipients must also verify who owns the key. An encrypted message can be read only by its recipients and by you, and it is always signed too. For both, you need a key of your own.

To sign or encrypt a message:

  1. In the message window, click Sign or Encrypt, or choose Main Menu > Digitally Sign or Encrypt. You can also press Ctrl+Shift+D or Ctrl+Shift+E. If no key is selected for your sending address, Inbox Classic offers to make one.
  2. Click Send.

While Encrypt is on, the line under the subject shows each recipient in a color with a picture, and Inbox Classic looks for the keys it does not have yet:

RecipientWhat it means
Green, with a lockYou verified the recipient’s key.
Blue, with a lockThe key is unverified, or it is your own key.
Gray, with a spinning circleInbox Classic is looking for the recipient’s key.
Amber, with a warning signNo key that can be used was found for the recipient.

If a recipient has no key

Encrypted sending requires a usable key for every recipient. If one does not, Send opens Recipients’ Keys. There you can click Find Key to look again, Remove from Message to leave that person out, or Send Without Encryption when no content from encrypted mail remains. You can also ask them for their public key and import it.

Replies to encrypted mail

A reply to an encrypted message, or a forward of one, starts with Encrypt on, and Inbox Classic asks before you turn it off. While the message contains text, pictures or attachments from encrypted mail, it can be sent only encrypted.

Drafts of an encrypted message are saved encrypted to your own key, without a digital signature. The sent message and its copy in Sent Items remain encrypted and signed.

Share your public key

To let someone send you encrypted mail, choose Main Menu > Attach My Public Key. Your public key is attached to the message as a file.
