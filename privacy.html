Privacy Policy

Effective 10 November 2026

Postal Systems collects no personal data through Inbox Classic. There are no accounts, no telemetry and no crash reports. Your mail and your passwords stay on your computer and with your own mail provider. This website counts visits with Plausible, which uses no cookies and keeps no personal data.

Who we are

Inbox Classic is made by Postal Systems OÜ (registry code 14971894), Narva mnt 5, 10117 Tallinn, Estonia. Questions about this policy: info@postalsys.com.

This website

inboxclassic.com is a static website hosted on GitHub Pages. It sets no cookies and loads no advertising, and its fonts, icons and images are served from the site itself. As the host, GitHub, Inc. processes technical data such as your IP address to deliver and protect the site, as described in the GitHub Privacy Statement. The app is downloaded from GitHub.

To see how many people visit and which downloads they choose, the site uses Plausible, an analytics tool without cookies that we run ourselves on our own server in the European Union (at a.inboxclassic.com). It records the page you visit, the page or site that sent you there, your browser, operating system and device type, your country, and the links and download buttons you click. Your IP address is used only to work out your country and, together with your browser, a daily changing code that counts unique visitors; neither is stored, and the code cannot be traced back to you. No data is shared with anyone else, and nothing is used for advertising or to follow you across websites.

The Inbox Classic app

Inbox Classic runs on your computer. It has no telemetry or crash reporting, and it sends Postal Systems nothing about you or your mail.

  • Your accounts, a copy of your mail for reading offline, its search index, the addresses you have written to and the AI agent log are kept in a folder on your computer: %LOCALAPPDATA%\InboxClassic on Windows, ~/Library/Application Support/Inbox Classic on the Mac and ~/.local/share/inbox-classic on Linux. Your settings are kept the way your system keeps them.
  • Your passwords and sign-in tokens are kept in your system's own password store: the Windows Credential Manager, the macOS keychain or, on Linux, the Secret Service (such as GNOME Keyring). If there is none, they are kept in memory only, until Inbox Classic quits.
  • Removing an account in Inbox Classic removes its mail copy and its password from your computer; the addresses remembered for address completion and the AI agent log stay until you clear them. Uninstalling Inbox Classic on Windows offers to remove the accounts and mail it kept; on the Mac and Linux the data folder stays until you delete it. Passwords of accounts still set up stay in your password store (under InboxClassic), where you can remove them. Your mail on your mail server is never removed.

Who the app talks to

Inbox Classic connects only to the services needed for the things you ask it to do:

  • Your mail servers, over encrypted connections, to read, send and organize your mail.
  • When you add an account, to find its server settings: DNS lookups over HTTPS through Cloudflare (with Google Public DNS as a fallback) and Mozilla's Thunderbird settings database receive the domain name of your address, for example example.com; your mail provider's own settings service may receive your address; and for an Exchange account, your organization's Autodiscover service may receive your address and password, as other mail programs send them.
  • When you sign in with Microsoft or Google (or with a sign-in app you added, whose provider Inbox Classic asks for its sign-in settings), you sign in on the provider's own page. The provider gives Inbox Classic your name, your e-mail address and tokens that let it read and send your mail over IMAP and SMTP. Inbox Classic uses them only to work with your own mailbox, keeps them only on your computer and shares them with nobody. You can withdraw this access at any time in your Microsoft or Google account settings.
  • Pictures from the internet in a message are loaded from the sender's servers only when you choose to see them. Links in messages open in your web browser.
  • Unsubscribing from a mailing list: when you click Unsubscribe and agree, Inbox Classic sends the list's own unsubscribe address the request the message asks for (this tells the list your address wants out), writes the message the list wants for you to send, or opens the list's page in your browser.
  • Brand logos: when your mail provider has checked that a message really comes from a brand and approved its logo (BIMI), Inbox Classic may fetch that logo from the brand's address once per run, without cookies, when you open the message.
  • Updates on the Mac: the Mac app checks once a day for a new version by reading the update list on this website (inboxclassic.com, hosted by GitHub) and downloads updates from GitHub. The check sends no information about you or your computer beyond what any web request does, such as your IP address. The Windows app does not check for updates.
  • Updates on Linux: the Linux app does not check for updates itself. If our apt repository is set up on your computer (you can add it yourself, and Linux packages newer than 0.9.0 add it when installed), your system's package manager fetches new versions from apt.inboxclassic.com, which we run on our own server in the European Union. The server keeps no record of these requests.

Inbox Classic's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Messages are shown with your system's own web component: the Microsoft Edge WebView2 Runtime on Windows, WebKit on the Mac and WebKitGTK on Linux. Any diagnostic data the WebView2 Runtime sends to Microsoft follows your Windows settings and the Microsoft Privacy Statement.

AI agents

There is no AI in Inbox Classic. If you use an AI agent of your own, such as Claude or Cursor, it can connect to Inbox Classic on your computer. The first time a program connects, you choose what it may do and which accounts it sees. Inbox Classic gives it the mail of those accounts on your computer only; what the agent does with it, including whether it sends it to an AI service, is up to the agent and its maker's terms. An agent allowed to organize mail can also unsubscribe from mailing lists for you. Every message an agent wants to send is shown to you first and needs your yes. Tools > AI Agents lists what each agent did, and there you can remove a program or turn agents off.

Your rights

As we hold no personal data about you, there is nothing for us to give you a copy of, correct or delete. Your data is on your computer and with your mail provider, where you control it. If you write to us, we use your message only to answer it.
